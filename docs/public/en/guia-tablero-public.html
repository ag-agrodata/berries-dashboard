How to read this guide

This dashboard is the public k-anonymous version of a real B2B commercial analytics case in the berry agro-industry. The original data is confidential with the client; what you see here is a derivative that does not allow identifying customers, persons, or transactions.

The guide is written for three audiences:

  • Recruiters and hiring managers who want to understand the reasoning behind the dashboard in 10 minutes.
  • Analysts who want to audit the decisions and the methodology.
  • Domain-curious readers who want to see how a real agro-industrial problem is translated into decision questions.
Project history: the case went through four phases before arriving here — working with the company → Excel + Tableau delivery → Streamlit replication → anonymization and publication. The full story is in the repository README.
00

Governance · how the data was protected

The first tab is not analytical: it is about governance. It is not an annex; it is part of the deliverable.

What it shows

  • Anonymization techniques applied: aggregation, k-anonymity (k ≥ 5), cell suppression for cells with < 10 contributors, category generalization, rounding, and perturbation.
  • Thresholds: k ≥ 5 and cells ≥ 10 on every published cell.
  • Transparency note: which categories were suppressed or generalized for size, and why.
  • Validation report: link to data/public/validacion.txt, with the re-identification attacks and the price-reconstruction test.

Why it is on the dashboard

A dashboard presented as "public with real data" has an obligation: to explain how the information was protected. Without this view, the dashboard would be a promise without a receipt. With it, the reader can audit the guarantee.

Lesson: governance is not a legal formality. It is what allows the case to be published without exposing the client. An analyst who knows how to anonymize is more valuable than one who only knows how to visualize.

Sales by channel · how are sales going and which channel is growing?

The commercial thermometer view. It answers the most basic committee question: are we growing or falling, and who is pushing us?

The three cards

MetricWhat it measuresWhy it matters
Total B2B net salesSum of net sales in the B2B universe over the periodCommercial health thermometer
Leading channelChannel with the highest net sales in the periodWhere sales are concentrated
Visible channelsChannels with ≥ 10 contributorsHow much of the universe can be shown without breaking k-anonymity

The key analytical decision

Retention alerts use price-neutral metrics in kilograms (decision D01 in the project brain). In this view, net sales are published in currency, but the retention traffic light is computed in kg. The reason: a price change must not make a customer look like they are buying less when they are buying the same volume.

Public note: the Retail channel (8 customers) is suppressed because it does not reach the 10-contributor threshold. The public analysis covers 2 of the 3 channels. The full reading exists only at the local pseudonymized level (decision D04).
02

Regional dynamics · where does revenue come from?

The commercial geography view. It answers: is revenue concentrated in the Central region, or distributed across the rest of the country?

The two views

  • Central vs Other distribution: donut with each macro-region's share of total sales.
  • Concentration by season: line with the Central region's percentage over time. Shows whether geographic dependence increases or decreases.

The key analytical decision

The 7 original regions were grouped into 2 macro-regions — "Central" (Bogotá + Sabana) and "Other regions" — because several individual regions did not reach 10 customers. The 3 customers with national coverage were diluted into "Other" to avoid singularization (decisions D05 and D06).

Public note: the public version does not distinguish Bogotá from Sabana, nor the individual regions within "Other". That is the price of anonymization: you gain protection, you lose granularity.
03

Alerts & risks · which customers are leaving?

The flagship view. The one projected in the committee. The one that turns the dashboard into a retention tool.

The business question

A B2B customer steadily reducing purchases is a churn in slow motion. Detecting it two months before it consolidates allows intervention with margin. Detecting it after the customer is gone is useless.

Decision D01 · kilograms, not currency

The alert is computed in kilograms. If it were computed in currency, a price drop would trigger a false churn alert even if the customer keeps buying the same volume. It is the most cited decision of the project because it avoids a classic bias.

Decision D02 · two consecutive months

A single-month drop does not trigger an alert. Two consecutive months are required to confirm a trend. The monthly seasonality of berries produces dips that are not churn signals. Windows of 1, 2 and 3 months were tested: one fired noise, three arrived late to the committee.

How to read the traffic light

LevelWhat it means
High riskDrop ≥ 30% sustained for 2 consecutive months
Medium riskDrop between 15% and 30%, sustained for 2 months
StableNormal purchase, or a drop that did not repeat

What the reader sees in the public version

Aggregates by risk level, not individual customers. Shown: how many customers are in each level, how many kg are at risk in total, and how the traffic light evolves month over month. The per-customer list exists only at the local pseudonymized level (decision D08).

Lesson: a useful alert is not the one that fires fastest, but the one that fires for the right reason. The 2-month window and the kg metric are the difference between a traffic light the committee uses and one they ignore.
04

Portfolio · which customers are strategic?

The strategic view. It answers: which customers buy only one species (cross-sell opportunity) and which concentrate revenue (strategic accounts)?

The four cards

  • Total customers in the active B2B universe.
  • Captive customers: those with ≥ 80% of volume in a single species (threshold D09).
  • High-value customers: those concentrating 80% of revenue (Pareto).
  • Pareto concentration: which bucket the index falls into (≤10%, 10-20%, 20-30%, >30%).

The key analytical decision

The Pareto index is published as a bucket, not as an exact number (decision D07). Publishing "N customers concentrate 80%" would allow inferring the base distribution. The bucket preserves the qualitative reading without exposing the fine data.

Public note: the customer × species matrix (the full version of the analysis) is not published. The distribution captive/diversified and global metrics are shown. The per-account operational view exists only at the local pseudonymized level (decision D08).

For the analyst auditing this case

If your role is to evaluate the quality of the analysis, these are the three artifacts to open, in order.

1 · cerebro-berries-public.md

Register of 14 decisions, 9 assumptions, glossary, 7 limitations and a roadmap. The document that lets you audit the reasoning without reading the code or accessing the original data. Start here.

2 · data/public/validacion.txt

Report of the 5 standard anonymization attacks (singularization, membership, attribute disclosure, identifiers, cells ≥ 10) plus the price-reconstruction test. The technical evidence of governance.

3 · src/berrix/app_public.py + live demo

Dashboard code and public demo on Streamlit Cloud. Lets you verify that the dashboard metrics match the documented decisions.

Questions a reviewer should ask

  • Do the four views answer real business questions, or are they visualization exercises?
  • Do decisions D01–D14 have sufficient justification? Which lack quantitative evidence (limitation L01)?
  • Does anonymization withstand the standard attacks? Are residual risks documented?
  • Does the public dataset allow reconstructing prices? (It should not, per the price test.)
  • Is the code reproducible? Does the README allow someone to clone and run without friction?
How to reproduce: uv sync and then uv run streamlit run src/berrix/app_public.py. pip alternative: pip install -r requirements.txt and streamlit run src/berrix/app_public.py.
